Same prediction now, different

 A small GPU experiment on transformer memory and density
 matrices



 Research and search date: 21 October 2026 (UTC). Prepared for a master's project. This is a
 proposed experiment, not a report of GPU results.

 Recommendation. Train one small transformer to remember a noisy clue while reading irrelevant
 tokens. Find pairs of histories that require exactly the same next-token probabilities, then append
 the same query and measure their different answers. Exchange saved memory between the
 histories. Test whether a tiny density matrix predicts the resulting behavior better than equally small
 classical models.

 The useful contribution would be a carefully measured test of whether a compact description
 remains accurate after memory interventions. Recovering predictive information, displaying belief
 geometry, and showing that internal memory affects predictions are already established ideas. The
 literature now includes especially close intervention experiments from September 2026.

 Treat the density matrix as an optional modeling constraint. For the generator recommended here,
 one ordinary probability already describes everything relevant about the hidden bit. A density matrix
 might help approximate the trained transformer's particular behavior, but this task provides no
 reason to expect a fundamental quantum advantage.

 What was actually done for this report. I searched primary papers and public repositories, read
 the three starting papers beyond their abstracts, inspected relevant analysis and intervention code,
 and checked the worked probabilities with exact rational arithmetic on a CPU. I also checked the
 proposed operator normalization numerically. I did not train a transformer, fit an encoder, run a GPU
 intervention, reproduce the papers, or benchmark runtime.

 Reading order. Sections 1-6 give the contribution, workflow, generator, settings, measurements,
 and overlap table. Appendix A gives the mathematics. Appendix B gives pseudocode. Appendix C
 records source access and code inspection. References are clickable.

 1. Candidate contribution and the closest work


 1.1 What the literature already establishes

 Riechers, Elliott, and Shai recover known predictive states from neural activations for classical,
 quantum, and post-quantum synthetic processes. Their states already describe distributions over
 complete futures. Their main analysis fits affine maps from concatenated last-position activations to
 known generator states; Appendix F describes probability weighting, deduplication, and
 cross-validation. Their comparisons include finite-order classical approximations. This is substantially
 more than recovering the current output distribution. It is not, however, the same test as fitting a
 small model of a frozen transformer's own future probabilities and testing it after partial cache
 exchanges. [1]

 Shai and colleagues make the next-token ambiguity particularly explicit with Random-Random-XOR.
 Known belief information can be recoverable across layers even when the final residual vector does
 not reveal it through the tested linear probe. A residual vector is the internal feature vector passed
 between transformer blocks. Their experiments already undermine the claim that a model trained on
 next-token prediction must represent only next-token probabilities. Their regression and training
 details appear in Appendix A. [2]

 Piotrowski and colleagues go beyond geometric recovery: they analyze the computations producing
 intermediate representations. Their constrained belief-update account predicts attention weights,
 value-output transformations, and embeddings in small transformers. Their appendices examine
 training, multiple layers, and architectural requirements. Thus, proposing to study how predictive
 representations update is also not automatically new. Their strongest mechanistic account has a
 specific small-model and generator scope. [3]

 The most important subsequent overlap is Balcells and colleagues, September 2026. They study
 belief representations in pretrained language models, intervene along directions that preserve the
 generator's complete next-token distribution, and investigate prediction changes under belief
 patching or steering. Their prediction interventions vary the edited suffix length and measure the
 final position's distribution. The inspected code evaluates a modified suffix using cached prefix
 information. This already covers belief interventions, next-token-invisible belief directions, and
 competition with unedited context. It does not establish the precise autonomous, compact-state,
 partial-cache transfer comparison proposed below. [4]

 An earlier public capstone by Balcells and García Segura also uses an encoder and decoder to inject
 alternative beliefs into a small Mess3 transformer and score next-token changes. It is relevant prior
 experimentation even though it is not a peer-reviewed paper. A learned decoder is not guaranteed to
 be a true inverse, and arbitrary decoded activations need not correspond to a natural history. [5]

 A probe is a fitted readout used to test what information can be recovered from internal numbers. A
 predictive state is a description sufficient for specified future predictions. A causal intervention
 changes part of a model's computation while holding other parts fixed. These are different tests.

 Predictive-state representations already define state using predictions of future observations and
 give recursive updates. Hidden quantum Markov models already use density matrices with
 probability-producing updates. Neither the probability chain rule nor a matrix update formula would
 be a contribution here. [6-8]

 Causal memory studies also precede this proposal. Othello-GPT edits internal board representations;
 cache-steering and Memory Inception alter saved attention information to change later behavior.
 Their existence rules out claiming the first causal use of transformer memory or the first useful cache
 intervention. [9-11]

 A public J-state closure research repository describes compact-state prediction, persistent-cache
 attribution, and intervention-retention protocols. Its own documentation separates implemented
 protocols from successful results. It is additional reason not to claim novelty for the broad idea of
 testing compressed states causally. [13]


 1.3 The narrow candidate

 Candidate contribution: On histories matched in their current full output distributions, quantify
 how well a three-real-number summary, fitted only on unmodified histories, predicts the frozen
 transformer's conditional future distributions after exchanging earlier-token versus current-token KV
 memory. Compare a two-dimensional density-matrix model with a four-state classical probabilistic
 model and a three-number recurrent neural model, under matched information and parameter
 budgets.

 KV memory means the keys and values saved by attention for previously processed tokens. Keys
 help choose which information to read; values carry the information being read. The important test is
 transfer from ordinary histories to interventions, including the direction and size of future changes.
 The density-matrix constraint is assessed through predictive errors, not appearance.

 Relative to [1-3], this targets the trained model's behavior, learned autonomous updates, and partial
 cache interventions. Relative to [4-5], it predicts several subsequent token steps using one initial
 compact summary, evaluates complete short-future distributions, and explicitly separates

 2. One numbered experimental workflow


 1. Write and verify the exact generator. Implement the rules in Section 3 and an oracle,
 meaning a program that computes the correct probabilities from those rules. Verify normalization,
 the matched pair, reset equivalence, and termination. Use exact fractions for the small worked
 example.

 2. Create disjoint history families before training. Assign all episodes sharing the same
 16-token irrelevant prefix to one split. Keep both cue values, every continuation, every reset version,
 and every intervention donor from that family in the same split. Reserve separate families for
 transformer training, transformer validation, analysis fitting, analysis validation, and final testing.

 3. Run a small correctness trial. Use one seed and 300 training steps. Verify decreasing loss,
 correct shifted targets, causal masking, padding, cache save/restore, and cached versus full
 recomputation. This trial is for implementation problems, not evidence about the research
 hypothesis.

 4. Train three small transformers normally, then freeze them. Use ordinary next-token
 cross-entropy, which penalizes assigning low probability to the observed next token. Select
 checkpoints using transformer validation only. Freeze all transformer weights before fitting any
 analysis tools.

 5. Check that each transformer learned the task. Compare its full next-token and short-future
 probabilities with the oracle. In particular, verify the noisy cue's delayed effect and the reset's
 removal of that effect. Do not assume that exact generator equalities imply exact model equalities.

 6. Collect three kinds of model information. Record the current token's final residual vector, its
 vectors across all layers, and the specified cache summary. Fit the same small density model and
 classical baselines separately to each source. Use model probabilities as the principal targets; use
 oracle beliefs only for a separate information-reading diagnostic.

 7. Test autonomous updates on ordinary histories. Encode a history once. Then give only
 identical continuation tokens, position, and the known sequence stage to the compact model.
 Compare its next distributions with transformer reference distributions. Never replace the predicted
 compact state with a fresh transformer encoding during the scored rollout.

 8. Apply the same cache-exchange intervention at two locations. For each matched pair,
 exchange all earlier positions, just the current position, or both. Keep positions, masks, lengths, and
 model seed fixed. Append identical continuations. Predict effects from the modified cache summary
 before revealing the reference results.

 3. One generator, including its own control


 3.1 Vocabulary and rules

 Use exactly eight tokens: ^, 0, 1, a, b, ?, R, $. Each symbol is one token; do not use a text tokenizer.

 An episode has this layout:


   ^  N[1] ... N[16]  C  W[1] ... W[d]  ?  Y  $

 The hidden bit Z is either 0 or 1 with equal probability. The 16 nuisance tokens N are independent
  fair draws from a and b; they reveal nothing about Z. The clue C equals Z with probability 0.8, and is
 the opposite bit with probability 0.2. Only C is shown to the transformer.

 After the clue, repeatedly choose the next token independently of Z:


   Next waiting-stage token                   Probability                 Effect


   a                                             0.4                      Keep Z and continue waiting


   b                                             0.4                      Keep Z and continue waiting


   R                                             0.1                        Replace Z by a new independent fair
                                                                                                      bit; continue waiting


   ?                                             0.1                       Leave the waiting stage; next emit the
                                                                                current Z as Y


 After Y, emit $ and end the episode. For fixed-horizon probability comparisons, regard $ as absorbing:
 once it is emitted, all remaining positions are $ by definition. Apply this stopping convention to the
 transformer and every surrogate as well. Do not use untrained transformer outputs after an end
 token.

 The observer's uncertainty about Z can be summarized by b = P(Z=1 | observed history). This is a
 posterior probability: a probability updated after seeing evidence. Immediately after clue 0, b=0.2;
 after clue 1, b=0.8. Fillers and ? do not change b. An R sets b=0.5. Thus one ordinary number plus
 the observable stage is enough for the true generator.

 The nuisance prefix supplies many genuinely different, label-independent histories for splitting and
 controls. It is not additional hidden structure. The clue always occupies zero-based position 17, after
 ^ and 16 nuisance tokens. All paired comparisons use the same nuisance prefix, waiting-token string,
 length, current token, and comparison position.

 Total variation distance, or TV, measures probability disagreement: add the absolute probability

 distributions. The one-token TV here is 0; the two-token joint-future TV is 0.06; the three-token TV is

 arithmetic while preparing this report.

 The control pair is h0R and h1R. Both now have a fresh fair Z, the same sequence stage, and the
 same waiting-token rules. Their distributions over every future sequence are identical, not merely
 their next-token distributions. Their previously computed cache tensors can still differ. Stored
 distinctions need not remain behaviorally relevant.

 This example uses elementary probability. It is a diagnostic construction, not a mathematical novelty
 claim.


 3.3 Difficulty and shortcut controls

 Keep the main experiment at clue reliability 0.8 and query probability 0.1. Difficulty can later be
 changed by lowering clue reliability or measuring longer delays, but do not introduce a parameter
 sweep in the first pilot.

 The delay is geometric: there is a 0.1 probability of a query on each waiting step. Generate episodes
 naturally and right-censor at context length 64; right-censoring means stopping observation without
 inventing an end or query token. Never force a query at the context boundary. Train the transformer
 on naturally occurring longer delays too. The primary longer-delay test withholds those lengths from
 the fitted analysis tools, not necessarily from the transformer.

 For controlled pairs, sample no-reset waiting strings from a and b, and evaluate at delays 4, 8, and
 12. Test analysis generalization separately at delays 24 and 32. Appending R gives the control at a
 common shifted position. All such strings have positive probability under the same generator. Label
 forced long no-reset histories as a stress-test distribution and report ordinary generator-weighted
 results separately.

 Balance the two clue values. Independently resample nuisance prefixes across families; keep each
 pair's nuisance prefix identical. Do not let filenames, split IDs, padding length, sample order, or
 position encode the clue. All models receive identical position and stage side information. Do not
 give a surrogate the true posterior, clue identity, reset flag, or future answer as an extra input.

 3.4 Data splits without analysis leakage

 Hash the 16-token nuisance string with a fixed documented hash and take its value modulo 100. Use
 buckets 0-69 for transformer training, 70-74 for transformer validation, 75-84 for analysis fitting,
 85-89 for analysis validation, and 90-99 for final testing. Hashing affects assignment only; it is never
 a model input. This grouping makes the evaluated post-clue histories disjoint. Shared very short
 prefixes before the clue are unavoidable and are not used as analysis examples.

 Generate training episodes online with independent random-number streams for each split and seed.
 Keep the tokenized history family, not individual activation rows, as the split unit. Deduplicate
 repeated analysis prefixes. Fit normalization, dimensionality reduction, encoders, update rules,
 diagnostic probes, thresholds chosen on validation, and any hyperparameter decisions only on their
 designated splits.

 Use 20,000 distinct starting histories for analysis fitting and 4,000 for analysis validation, balanced
 by clue, reset status, and delays 0-12. Collect their ordinary continuation states for fitting update
 consistency. The final ordinary test uses 1,024 new nuisance families, paired clues, and a
 prespecified mixture of delays 4, 8, and 12. Use 512 further families for delays 24 and 32. A fixed
 hash selects 256 ordinary-test pairs and 128 long-delay pairs for complete three-token trees. Keep
  all final families sealed until settings are fixed.

 The group split slightly changes the nuisance-prefix sampling distribution within each split. This
 cannot change the exact post-clue oracle probabilities because nuisance strings are independent of
 Z. Scientific scoring starts after the clue, not on the split-conditioned nuisance generator.

 4.3 The density state and its updates, in words

 Use a 2-by-2 complex density matrix. A density matrix is a Hermitian matrix (equal to its conjugate
 transpose), positive semidefinite (no negative eigenvalues), with trace one (diagonal entries sum to
 one). It needs three independent real coordinates. Constrain the encoder's three outputs to the
 inside of a unit ball and convert them to the matrix using Appendix A. This guarantees validity; it
 does not guarantee useful predictions.

  Fit small token-specific matrices that jointly produce the next-token probabilities and update the
 density after an observed token. These are Kraus operators: matrices whose normalized products
 ensure that the state remains valid and that output probabilities add to one. The operators are fitted
 to the frozen transformer's probabilities, not imported from the known generator.

 Use two trainable complex 2-by-2 operators per token, plus a tiny fixed numerical regularization
 branch. Their raw entries have an intercept and a linear dependence on t/64. Use a separate
 operator set for each of four observable stages: waiting, answer, end-token pending, and
 invalid-prefix. A fifth stopped stage is fixed and emits only $. The stage is a deterministic counter
 updated from supplied tokens, not hidden information. It is supplied equally to every baseline and
 counted as part of stored state.

 The tested compact state is the tuple (density matrix, stage, position), not the matrix alone. This is a
 small, explicitly stage- and position-conditioned quantum-instrument surrogate, not a claim that the
 transformer is a quantum system or that its whole computation is a stationary two-dimensional

 HQMM. An HQMM, or hidden quantum Markov model, is a sequence model whose hidden state is a
 density matrix and whose symbol operations generate probabilities and conditional updates. [7-8]

 There are 1,024 real fitted operator parameters: 4 stages x 2 time coefficients x 8 tokens x 2
 operators x 8 real entries. Together with the encoder, the matched density model has 5,251 fitted
 parameters, excluding common preprocessing. No extra readout network is fitted; the operators
 themselves supply the probabilities.

  Fit the encoder and operators together after freezing the transformer. Use Adam, learning rate
 0.001, betas (0.9,0.999), no weight decay, clipping at 1, batch 256, and 3,000 updates. Use three
 initialization seeds per transformer/source/representation; choose using analysis validation and
 report restart variability. This is a small fit, not another transformer training run.

 The loss matches full eight-token teacher distributions at rollout offsets 0-4, plus 0.1 times squared
 update-consistency distance to a freshly encoded ordinary continuation state. A teacher is the frozen
 transformer being approximated. The fresh encoding is used only as a training target and diagnostic,
 never as a replacement rollout state. Use equal weight per rollout offset and per clue/reset stratum.

 Half the fitting continuations are sampled from the generator; half are supported strings with a query
 placed uniformly at one of the first four positions, with fillers drawn fairly and the subsequent bit
 drawn from the oracle conditional distribution. These are observations supplied to the model, not
 internal interventions. Collect their teacher probabilities using the full normal prefix. Fit no
 parameters on patched caches or final-test histories.

 For the density model, consistency distance is trace distance, defined in Appendix A. Use total
 variation between state probabilities for the classical HMM and Euclidean distance divided by
 2*sqrt(3) for the bounded three-number neural state. Squaring these distances gives a comparable
 bounded consistency penalty. These geometries remain different modeling choices; report the
 penalty and do not treat it as evidence of quantum mechanics.

 5. Measurements, interventions, and predefined
 decisions


 5.1 Comparing histories properly

 Let P_G be the exact generator distribution, Q_T the trained transformer's distribution, and Q_D the
 compact surrogate's distribution. Keep these names separate in code and figures. Compare the
 entire eight-token distributions, including probability incorrectly placed on impossible tokens.

 The same sampled token only means two random draws happened to agree. The same most likely
 token only means their largest-probability entries agree. Neither implies equality of the full
 distributions. In this experiment, pair matching uses TV between complete Q_T distributions, not
 sampled tokens or argmax labels.

 First generate candidate pairs by the oracle rules. For the model-matched subset require
 TV(Q_T(.|h0), Q_T(.|h1)) <= 0.01. Freeze this tolerance before inspecting intervention results. Report
 the fraction retained and report results for all oracle-matched pairs as well; a tiny selected subset
 would be misleading. At least 90% of the ordinary pairs should qualify for a clean pilot.

 For horizons 1, 2, and 3, enumerate all 8^k token strings, using the absorbing-end convention.
 Multiply conditional probabilities along each branch to obtain the joint distribution. Do not substitute
 separate token marginals for the joint. Include invalid-prefix branches from the model's own
 probabilities; do not renormalize away their mass. The surrogate's invalid-prefix stage makes its
 recurrence defined on these branches too, even though their behavior is poorly constrained by
 natural data.

 At longer horizons, avoid an exponentially large tree. Test distributions along fixed, shared supported
 continuations and report probabilities of a prespecified small set of strings. Such a set is not a
 complete joint-future distribution. Use continuations ?, a b ?, and (a b)^3 ? for the causal endpoints,
 plus randomly sampled supported eight-token continuations for update testing.

   00: recipient baseline                           Recipient                     Recipient

 5.4 How to interpret outcomes

 Useful memory finding, density not helpful: The transformer passes learning checks; partial
 swaps causally alter delayed predictions; the cache-based classical state predicts those effects as
 well as or better than the density model. Continue the memory investigation with the simpler model.
 This is not a failed project.

 Evidence that the density constraint helps here: The density model passes ordinary, update,
 delay, and intervention tests and beats both capacity-matched and time-matched classical fits
 consistently. This supports this inductive bias, meaning this restriction on the kinds of models that
 can be learned, for this task and budget. It does not demonstrate quantum computation or an
 irreducible quantum memory advantage.

 Revise: The transformer is inaccurate, cache parity fails, reset interventions damage behavior, or all
 compact models fail even on ordinary histories. Those failures do not test the intended scientific
 question cleanly. If the native-cache posterior probe succeeds but its PCA version fails, revise
 preprocessing rather than declaring memory absent.

 Evidence against this particular approach: The density model is valid but predicts poorly,
 cannot update without repeated teacher access, works only on fitting histories, loses to matched
 classical models, or mispredicts genuine held-out cache interventions despite successful ordinary
 prediction. A representation may be decodable yet not a sufficient causal summary. Do not fit
 intervention outcomes after inspecting final tests and call the resulting performance held out.

 Appendix A. Optional mathematics


 A1. Probability definitions and the oracle

 For any distribution p and q over a finite set X,


   TV(p,q) = (1/2) sum_{x in X} |p(x)-q(x)|.
   KL(p||q) = sum_{x:p(x)>0} p(x) log[p(x)/q(x)].

 KL is Kullback-Leibler divergence, an asymmetric discrepancy measured here using natural
 logarithms. It is supplementary to TV. Generator-zero terms contribute zero; do not renormalize Q_T
 to the allowed vocabulary subset.

 With a fair hidden Z and clue reliability r=0.8, Bayes' rule gives P(Z=1|C=1)=r and P(Z=1|C=0)=1-r.
  All waiting emissions are independent of Z. A reset resamples Z independently, proving equality of
  all future distributions from h0R and h1R. This is a standard conditioning argument.

 For a model Q and a continuation w=(x1,...,xk),


   Q(w|h) = product_{j=1..k} Q(xj | h,x1,...,x[j-1]).

 This chain rule is not a new result. In the worked example, differences in two-token futures occur
 only at ? 0 and ? 1, each with absolute difference 0.06, yielding TV=0.06. At horizon three, a query
 can occur at the first step (contribution 0.06) or after a non-reset filler (contribution 0.8*0.06=0.048),
 yielding 0.108.

 For the generator, a sufficient waiting-stage state is (b,stage), where b is the posterior. It is not
 necessarily sufficient for the learned transformer's small history-dependent errors. That distinction
 motivates using Q_T, not P_G, as the surrogate target.


 A2. Constructing the two-dimensional density

 The fitted encoder gives u in R^3. Let r = u / sqrt(1 + u.u). Then ||r|| < 1. Construct


   rho(r) = (1/2) [[1+r_z,   r_x - i*r_y],
                   [r_x+i*r_y, 1-r_z     ]].

 Here i is the imaginary unit. The eigenvalues are (1+||r||)/2 and (1-||r||)/2, so the matrix is positive
 and has trace one. These three real coordinates are often called a Bloch vector; this name does not
 imply that the neural model uses a physical qubit.

 The exact generator can be represented with rho_G=diag(1-b,b). Its off-diagonal entries are zero in
 one fixed basis. Therefore the present task does not require noncommuting quantum states.
 Noncommuting means that exchanging the order of matrix multiplication changes the result.
 Off-diagonal entries in a fitted basis alone do not establish nonclassical information; a common basis
 change can alter them.

 For two such matrices, trace distance is


   D_tr(rho,sigma) = (1/2) ||rho-sigma||_1
                   = (1/2) ||r(rho)-r(sigma)||_2,

 where the first norm is the sum of singular values and the second is ordinary Euclidean length. The
 oracle pair has trace distance 0.6 despite identical next-token distributions; after reset it is zero.
 These facts describe this representation, not the trained model's unknown state.

 A3. Valid learned probabilities and updates

 For stage s and position t, form two raw complex 2-by-2 matrices per token:


   B[s,x,j](t) = B0[s,x,j] + (t/64)*B1[s,x,j], j=1,2.
   B[s,x,3](t) = sqrt(epsilon/8) * I, epsilon=1e-6.
   G = sum_{x,j} B[s,x,j](t)^dagger B[s,x,j](t).
   K[s,x,j](t) = B[s,x,j](t) G^(-1/2).

 Dagger means conjugate transpose. The fixed third branch makes G positive definite. Compute its
 inverse square root with a stable 2-by-2 Hermitian eigendecomposition; do not clip an invalid matrix
 and silently claim exact normalization. Because all branches are included,


   sum_{x,j} K[s,x,j]^dagger K[s,x,j] = I.
   A_x(rho) = sum_j K[s,x,j] rho K[s,x,j]^dagger.
   q_D(x|rho,s,t) = trace[A_x(rho)].
   U_x(rho,s,t) = A_x(rho) / q_D(x|rho,s,t).

 The probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one, and the conditional updated state is valid. The
 readout can equivalently be written trace(E_x rho), where E_x=sum_j K_xj^dagger K_xj. Each E_x is
 positive and the E_x sum to I. These are standard quantum-instrument equations. [7-8,16]

 For differentiable implementation, a useful 2-by-2 formula avoids differentiating eigenvectors near
 repeated eigenvalues. Let a=sqrt(det(G)) and c=sqrt(trace(G)+2a). Then G^(-1/2)=c*(G+a*I)^(-1).
 Compute real traces and determinants after checking Hermitian symmetry; use double precision for
 these tiny normalizations if needed. This is a standard matrix identity, not a proposed contribution.

 The stage update is deterministic: waiting plus ? becomes answer; waiting plus a,b,R remains waiting;
 answer plus 0 or 1 becomes end-pending; end-pending plus $ becomes stopped. Every other non-end
 transition becomes invalid. In the invalid stage, $ stops and all other tokens remain invalid. For the
 stopped stage, emit $ with probability one. The position increments by one until the context
 boundary. This specifies all branches without leaking the cue into the side information.

 The generator itself admits a diagonal realization. In the waiting stage use sqrt(0.4)I for each filler,
 sqrt(0.1)I for ?, and two reset branches sqrt(0.05)I and sqrt(0.05)X, where X exchanges basis states 0
 and 1. On diagonal densities the reset output is I/2 after normalization. In the answer stage use the
 two basis projectors for tokens 0 and 1; in end-pending emit $. This construction is only a correctness
 reference; do not use its known parameters to fit the transformer surrogate.


 A4. Classical alternatives and costs

 For the four-state classical model, let b be a four-entry probability column vector. For each stage and
 position, normalize raw scores over (token x, destination j) separately for each source i:


   M_x[j,i] = softmax_{x,j}(A0[s,x,j,i] + (t/64)*A1[s,x,j,i]).
   q_C(x|b,s,t) = 1^T M_x b.
   b_next = M_x b / q_C(x|b,s,t).

  All entries are nonnegative and sum_{x,j}M_x[j,i]=1. Therefore the probabilities and conditional
 updates are valid. There are 4*2*8*4*4=1,024 raw parameters. The density model's 1,024 raw
 parameters include complex real/imaginary entries; comparing just Hilbert dimension 2 against four
 classical states would obscure their equal three-real-number state storage.

 The density computations involve complex products and a small inverse square root, so they may
 cost more despite equal parameter counts. Report this directly. No conclusion here concerns
 physical quantum memory, quantum speedup, or infinite-precision computational advantages.

 A5. Fitting and evaluating state closure

 For one root h, start with z0=D(F(S_h)), where F is the fixed source extraction/preprocessing.
 Recursively update z[k+1]=U_{x[k+1]}(z[k],s[k],t[k]) using only supplied tokens and deterministic
 side information. Train with


   L = mean_{k=0..4} KL(Q_T(.|h,x[1:k]) || q(.|z[k],s[k],t[k]))
       + 0.1 * mean_{k=1..4} d(z[k], stop_gradient(D(F(S_hx[1:k]))))^2.

 Use balanced sampling as specified in Section 4. Targets and clean encodings may be precomputed;
 the transformer remains frozen. The stop-gradient operation prevents the consistency term from
 moving its current target in the same optimization step. Each target encoding also receives ordinary
 supervised losses when its prefix appears as an example. Stop at episode termination rather than
 treating padding as data.

 At evaluation there are no teacher refreshes. Separately report one-step updates initialized from a
 fresh state and 4-/8-step updates initialized only once. The latter test is harder. Fitting a decoder at
 every freshly observed transformer state does not establish it.

 For ordinary states at a common position and stage, a valid instrument gives an important check: the
 TV distance between its complete k-token future distributions cannot exceed the initial trace
 distance. A complete future string is one outcome of a combined measurement. This is an
 established distinguishability bound, not a proposed discovery. It does not apply in the same form to
 distributions conditioned on a rare forced continuation; conditioning can amplify differences.

 When comparing true model futures to surrogate geometry, the triangle inequality adds
 approximation error:


   TV(Q_T^k(h),Q_T^k(h'))
    <= TV(Q_T^k(h),Q_D^k(h)) + D_tr(rho_h,rho_h')
       + TV(Q_D^k(h'),Q_T^k(h')).

 Thus matrix separation alone is insufficient; the two prediction-error terms must be measured.
 Short-horizon agreement also does not prove equality of all future distributions.


 A6. Causal endpoint and its scope

 For a fixed continuation c ending in ?, define the effect of partial swap a as


   Delta_T(a,c) = Q_T(1 | edited_cache_a, supplied c)
                  - Q_T(1 | recipient_cache, supplied c).

 Predict Delta_D from the two initial cache encodings and their autonomous updates. Compare signed
 error, absolute error, and direction, alongside full-distribution effects. A hybrid cache does not have a
 generally defined generator posterior, so do not score it against an invented P_G target.

 For interaction, report f11-f10-f01+f00, where f is the answer probability in each cache arm. This is a
 standard two-factor contrast. It reveals non-additivity; it is not a percentage of information located in
 either block.

 The intervention changes persistent variables after the current logits exist. Hence the tested
 estimand is the distribution after one or more externally supplied tokens. This avoids attributing a
 change to an output that was already computed before intervention.
